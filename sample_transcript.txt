CFO REMARKS — AMY HOOD
================================================================================

REVENUE AND OPERATING INCOME:
We delivered results that exceeded expectations across revenue, operating income,
and earnings per share, reflecting strong execution and growing demand for the
Microsoft Cloud.

Total revenue: $82.9 billion, up 18% year-over-year, beating consensus of $81.4B.
Microsoft Cloud revenue: $54.5 billion, up 29% year-over-year — the first time
Microsoft Cloud has crossed $50 billion in a single quarter.

Segment breakdown:
- Intelligent Cloud (Azure, server products, GitHub): $34.68 billion, up 30%
- Productivity and Business Processes (Office, LinkedIn, Dynamics): $35.01 billion, up 17%
- More Personal Computing: declined year-over-year

Azure growth: 40% year-over-year (39% in constant currency). Q2 guidance was
37-38% constant currency — Azure came in above the high end.

MARGINS:
Gross margin: 67.6%, the narrowest since 2022, as depreciation costs mounted
in connection with our data center infrastructure build-out.
Operating margin Q4 guidance: approximately 44%, down from 46.3% in Q3.
Microsoft Cloud gross margin Q4 guidance: approximately 64%, down year-over-year.

CAPITAL EXPENDITURE:
Q3 capex including finance leases: $31.9 billion, up 49% year-over-year.
This came in below the $34.9 billion analyst consensus.

For calendar year 2026, we expect to invest roughly $190 billion in capital
expenditures. This includes approximately $25 billion from the impact of higher
component pricing driven by the global memory crunch related to AI demand.
Q4 capex guidance: over $40 billion in the quarter.

We remain confident in the return on these investments, given higher demand
signals and increasing product usage, as well as the efficiencies we are already
driving across the platform — including 40% inference improvements.

Two-thirds of our capex relates to short-lived assets (servers, GPUs, CPUs),
with the remainder in longer-lived infrastructure (land, buildings).

CAPACITY AND DEMAND:
Despite these investments and continued efforts to bring GPU, CPU, and storage
capacity online faster, we expect to remain supply-constrained at least through
2026. We continue to balance first-party usage with Azure customer demand.

We expect Azure growth to show modest acceleration in the second half of
calendar year 2026 compared with the first half.

BACKLOG AND FORWARD VISIBILITY:
Commercial remaining performance obligations: $627 billion, up 99% year-over-year.
This is the largest backlog Microsoft has ever reported — it doubled in a single year.

OUTLOOK — FY2027:
Management guided for continued double-digit revenue and operating income growth
into FY2027, with capex rising above $40 billion in Q4 to meet persistent
AI-driven demand.

================================================================================
ANALYST Q&A
================================================================================

Q (Brent Thill, Jefferies): One of the big pushbacks we get is that AI is going
to be really expensive. How do you think about ROI on the $190B capex?

A (Amy Hood): We remain confident in the return. Higher demand signals,
increasing product usage, and efficiency gains like 40% inference improvements
all support the investment case. The $25 billion component pricing impact is
largely pass-through cost driven by the global memory environment.

Q (Analyst): Can you give more colour on Azure acceleration into H2 CY2026?

A (Amy Hood): We are getting better visibility into our ability to put pressure
on efficiencies — speeding up deliveries into data centres and making capacity
revenue-ready as quickly as possible. The pressure between first-party usage and
Azure demand will persist, but we're doing our best to bring supply online faster,
hence the capex ramp in H2.
